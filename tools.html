-ing

Tools for making things. Drafting, Sliding, Working, Lighting, Coxpit, Planning, Giting, Grouping. Grouping is the odd one: it puts the others to work as team members.

우리말

Apps for using, not making. 닿기 · 바짝 · 또봄. Korean names, because they are for a day rather than a workshop.

IN DETAIL
DraftingAIP·001
AI writes proposals.
You write the document.

An idea interview turns into a PRD, feature spec and IA. Every AI sentence arrives as a suggestion — only what you accept becomes the document.

Drafting · document + suggestions
SlidingAIP·002
Say it.
It becomes a deck.

A conversational slide studio on your local Claude subscription. Four questions, a delivery plan, then one slide at a time for your approval — your words become deck-wide rules. No credits, the deck stays on your machine.

Open ↗Download ↓
Sliding · cockpit
CoxpitAIP·003
Own your agent fleet.

A self-hosted cockpit for a fleet of AI coding agents across your own machines. Parallel worktree runs, a live board, compare & merge, and a web terminal. Your code never leaves your network.

Open ↗Code
coxpit · fleet console live
LightingAIP·004
AI writes your chapters.
You write the book.

An AI e-book studio. An interview becomes chapter drafts; you edit, add a cover, and export to EPUB3 and PDF — validated and self-hosted, on your machine.

Lighting · manuscript + suggestions ● building
WorkingAIP·005
Start with one question,
not a blank page.

A macOS app for planning a program end to end — understand, plan, run, wrap up. The AI proposes; nothing is created until you accept. Every document lands as a real .md file in your own folder. No account, no server.

Open ↗Download ↓
Working · propose + accept
GroupingAIP·006
Your products,
working as your team.

The hub where AIP Lab tools become team members. Mention a member in a channel and its product does the work in its own workshop. Results arrive as proposal cards, you accept, and accepted work relays to the next member on one hash-chained log.

Open ↗Download ↓
Grouping · request, proposal, relay
GitingAIP·007
Stars are the past.
Clone it and measure.

An open-source curation site that reads repos by measurement, not stars. Every report starts from a real clone — lines of code, commit density, tests, the license text — drafted by AI, checked against the probe numbers, and published only after human review. Agents read it too, over MCP.

Open ↗Threads
Giting · measured reports
PlanningAIP·008
The agent plans the day.
You approve and adjust.

A PM app for one person running more than one project. Open it and today's order is already laid out, each block with a one-line reason. It runs on rules alone; add an AI key and it gets sharper. Everything the agent does can be undone, and it all lives in one local file. No server, no account.

Open ↗Download ↓
Planning · today, with reasons
DatgiAIP·009
Run today.
Get closer to somewhere.

An iOS app that turns runs into a journey across a map. It does not follow you while you run: no GPS, no live tracking. You enter the distance afterwards and the marker moves, and the cities along the way stand as milestones. Seoul to Busan is 418 km, reached one run at a time. Everything stays on the phone, with no account and no server.

Open ↗Privacy
Datgi · a run becomes distance
BajjakAIP·010
An hour ends
with a decision.

A macOS app you keep open while a meeting runs. A one-line purpose becomes agenda items, and each one carries the condition that ends it: running out of time is not the same as being done. When the hour is up the minutes are already written, ready to paste into Slack. The app has no colour, so going over time inverts half the screen instead. No recording, no account, no server.

Open ↗
Bajjak · pressure left, outcome right
TtobomAIP·011
You save things.
You never open them again.

An iOS app that brings back what you saved. Share a link from any app and it lands in Ttobom with its title and preview already fetched. No folders, no tags, nothing to organise — forgetting is allowed. Each day it picks a few things out for you: saved long ago, untouched for a while, and still worth a look. Everything is computed on the phone, with no account and no server.
